ExeTix · how it works

Get there first. Or get the spare.

Follow the nights you go to. ExeTix watches their ticket pages, pings you when they move, and runs a student marketplace for when you miss out.

Try it on the web ↗ Start with drops ↓

Coming to the App Store · in TestFlight beta now

1 · Drop alerts

Pinged the second tickets go live.

Follow any Exeter club, and any Guild society or AU club. ExeTix watches their ticket pages and tells you when something changes:

  • AnnouncedA new night is listed, so you know it is coming.
  • 10 min warningWhen the release time is known, a heads-up so you are logged in and ready.
  • DroppedTickets are live. One tap opens the ticket page.
  • RestockA sold-out night reopens, usually because a reservation lapsed. These go in about ninety seconds.
  • Resale listedA student lists a spare on ExeTix for a night you want.

On iPhone, drop alerts are time-sensitive, so they break through Focus. They carry the night’s flyer and come with buttons: Get tickets, View night, Sell. A tap goes straight to the ticket page, even from a cold launch.

The alert kinds, as the app introduces them.

The Drops tab

A radar for every night you follow.

Your next drop sits at the top with a live countdown, then what is dropping soon, what is live now, and every sold-out night being watched for a restock.

Set a reminder on a night that drops within eight hours and the countdown moves onto your Lock Screen and into the Dynamic Island as a Live Activity. The Tonight in Exeter widget puts the big night’s flyer, start time and resale price on your home screen.

2 · Resale

Sold out? Buy a student’s spare.

Every seller verifies an @exeter.ac.uk email and lists under their real name, and the name on the ticket has to match the account. Listing a spare takes about a minute: pick the night, set a price, add the ticket.

The buyer pays through Stripe. The ticket’s QR code is released to them and waits in the Tickets tab for the door. If it fails at the door, a photo or video from the door gets a full refund.

Sold-out nights get an I want a ticket button. Tap it and you are pinged the moment someone lists one, and sellers can see how many people want their night.

The fees

Sellers pay £0. Buyers pay what the card costs.

Sellers keep 100% of their price. Buyers pay one booking fee, and it is only the processing: the smallest amount that covers the card fee on the payment plus the payout fee on the seller’s transfer. That comes to about a third of a typical student-resale booking fee.

3 · Auto-buy

Set a price. Let it buy the spare.

On ExeTix Auto, arm any night with a maximum all-in price. When a resale listing on ExeTix appears at or under it, the server buys it for you with your saved card, the QR lands in Tickets, and a push tells you it is done.

One ticket per club per night per account. And it only ever buys ExeTix resale listings: buying on another ticketing platform automatically would break that platform’s terms, so it never does. ExeTix resale is the one market ExeTix runs itself.

4 · Referrals

Share your code. Get paid monthly.

  • Your friendEnters your code or taps your link, and gets £2 off booking fees.
  • When they joinYou get a free month.
  • Every month£2 for each friend on a paid plan, for as long as they stay.
  • Three friendsCover your own plan. Every one after that is cash.
  • Cash outFrom £5, straight to your bank.

Referral cash comes out of subscription revenue, not ticket fees, which stay at cost.
