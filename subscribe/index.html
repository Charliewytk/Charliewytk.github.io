Why the annualised number is the wrong number

A 3% spread held to the median 49-day close annualises to 24.6% a year. That is how this kind of thing is normally sold, and it is worthless.

24.6%a year — what a 3% spread over 49 days looks like when you annualise it
+1.38%per deal — after a 2.4% break rate, a 27% median loss when one breaks, 0.40% round-trip cost and 0.49% of forgone T-bill carry

Price a 1% spread the same way and it is −0.57% per deal. Break-even sits at 1.59%. That is the number the product publishes, not the annualised one.

The break rate is 2.4% after a definitive proxy — 39 of 1,606 settled outcomes. It is a floor, not a ceiling: deals that die before that stage never enter the sample, so risk from announcement is higher. Carry is the 13-week bill, fetched rather than assumed. US/GBP round-trip FX and spread are charged in the same pass.
