Privacy

Last updated 11 August 2026 · Doorly is in beta

Doorly needs to know where you are starting from and where you are staying, because that is the entire point of the product. This page explains exactly what happens to that information. There are no accounts, no advertising, and nothing is sold.

Who is responsible

Doorly is an independent beta project run by Charlie Wuytack, the data controller for the purposes of UK GDPR. Contact: charlie@wuytack.net.

What Doorly collects

DataWhyWhere it goes
Addresses you type To turn text into coordinates Doorly's server, then a geocoding provider (LocationIQ, or OpenStreetMap's Nominatim)
Your precise location, only if you tap "use my current location" To fill in the starting address for you Sent once to Doorly's server and its geocoding provider to get a place name back
Coordinates of your start and destination To find nearby airports and estimate ground transport OpenRouteService, for travel times and distances
Airport codes and travel dates To price flights The flight price provider (Travelpayouts/Aviasales)
Your IP address Rate limiting, so one visitor cannot exhaust shared API quota Held in memory on Doorly's server; it may also appear in short-lived host logs

Flight providers never receive your address. They only ever see an airport pair and a date. The link between "where you live" and "which airport" is worked out on Doorly's side and does not leave it.

What is stored, and for how long

  • In your browser only: your last search, so the page is not empty when you come back, and any API keys you enter yourself in the "connect" dialog. These live in your browser's local storage and are never sent to Doorly. Clearing your browser data removes them.
  • On Doorly's server, as a cache: address lookups (7 days), route times and distances (30 days), reverse-geocoded place names (30 days) and flight prices (1–6 hours). This cache is keyed by the query itself, not by who asked, and is not linked to you or your IP address.
  • Coordinates from "use my current location" are rounded to roughly 110 metres before they are used as a cache key, so the cache does not hold a precise record of where anyone stood.

Cookies and tracking

Doorly sets no cookies and runs no advertising or analytics trackers. It uses local storage for the two things listed above, which stays on your device.

If error monitoring is enabled, crash reports may be sent to Sentry to keep the site working. Those reports have query strings stripped from every URL before sending — so the addresses and dates in your search are not included — and session replay and performance tracing are switched off.

Booking links

When you click through to book, you leave Doorly for an airline or a travel agent. Some of those links are affiliate links, meaning Doorly may earn a commission if you book. The destination site sets its own cookies and applies its own privacy policy, which Doorly does not control. Affiliate links never change the ranking of results and never change the price you pay.

Third parties your data reaches

  • Geocoding: LocationIQ, or OpenStreetMap Foundation (Nominatim)
  • Routing: OpenRouteService (HeiGIT)
  • Flight prices: Travelpayouts (Aviasales)
  • Error monitoring, if enabled: Sentry
  • The hosting provider that runs the site

Your rights

Under UK GDPR you can ask what is held about you, ask for it to be corrected or deleted, and complain to the ICO. In practice Doorly holds no account and no profile, so there is usually nothing to return beyond what is described above. Email charlie@wuytack.net and it will be dealt with.

Location access is always your choice. Your browser asks before sharing it, you can refuse, and typing an address instead works exactly as well.

Children

Doorly is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect their data.

Changes

This page will change as the product does. The date at the top always reflects the current version.
