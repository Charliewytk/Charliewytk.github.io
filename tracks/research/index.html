The kill log

Research that says no.

Most trading ideas die the moment they meet a realistic cost model or an honest holdout. I built the tooling to find that out early, and I publish the kills.

Nine real, zero tradeable

In the first programme, about 110 hypotheses across equities, ETFs, Polymarket, factors and filings produced nine real anomalies. None were bigger than the cost of trading them.

Polymarket's longshot bias is real at about 2¢, and the spread is 1.5–3¢. Dip-buying made +20bp in 2016–21 and +2bp in 2021–26, below costs. 25 equity factors that survived multiple-testing correction turned out to be one levered beta bet wearing 29 hats.

The method

  1. Power checkWork out the smallest effect the data could detect before searching.
  2. Pre-registerRules, split date, bars and costs, hashed before any outcome is read.
  3. Develop in-sampleTime splits with an embargo; CIs clustered by week, month and coin.
  4. One-shot holdoutA lock file is written before the first label is read. One look.
  5. Attack itIndependent reviews for leakage, costs and recency, plus deflation.
  6. Paper, then a gateA forward paper book behind an e-process. A person decides.

A retraction, in full

ClaimedBuying a newly migrated token 1–2 seconds after the snipers had positive expected value. It replicated and passed a null test.
Caught88 of 1,320 tick files were sparse, so entries were priced before the snipers moved the price. Those files alone made the result.
CorrectedOn the 1,232 complete pools, entry loses at every delay: −5.2% a trade at 1 s. Verdict RED, published the same day.
FixedThe loader now rejects sparse pools, and the model that learned the artefact was retired.
