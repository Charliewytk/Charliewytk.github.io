Track 03 · on-chain infrastructure

Solana execution

A third of a millisecond, and why it didn't matter.

Newly graduated tokens move fastest in their first seconds on a pool. I wanted to know how much of a bad exit is latency and how much is the market, so I built the fast path first and measured it.

What is built

Exact pricing. The pump AMM's Anchor IDL is fetched on-chain and pinned. Pool accounts and swap events are decoded with Borsh, and quotes use integer constant-product maths on the real reserves, fee tier included (62 of 62 tiers matched).

Streaming triggers. Stops, take-profits, trailing and clock exits, insider sells and rug labels are evaluated on every transaction, not on a polling timer.

Pre-armed exits. The sell is assembled at entry. At the trigger only the amount and minimum-out are filled in. The transaction builder runs in 0.035 ms at p50 with the pool cached.

The result that mattered

The dry pilot's stops were filling well past their −12% line. The obvious suspect was latency, so the streaming, pre-armed engine cut that path to a third of a millisecond. Replayed on the same positions, it improved the average stop fill by 0.4 points.

One sell jumps the pool straight through the stop, and being faster cannot recover a price that never traded. That answer saved the next obvious spend: renting a server next to the validators.
