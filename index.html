PowerLeash

Apple's low power mode made my Mac worse, so I made my own

A custom low power mode. Apple's native low power mode made macOS feel sluggish: the cursor was slow and laggy and the movements felt off.

I also only have a charger of 45W on a Mac that can draw much more than that: I was losing battery working on heavy tasks while on charge.

I found out the reason LPM was sluggish was because it killed the CPU by about 40%.

Eco Mode is my own low power mode, moving all background apps to efficiency cores leaving the foreground untouched. Match Charger capped my total draw by what I could supply.

Merger Monitor

The one with a price on it

Every US cash merger that has reached a definitive proxy, priced against the live market, carrying the sentence from the SEC filing the deal price came from.

Break-even is 1.59% today and eleven of the sixteen live deals clear it. The rest are published anyway, because a wide spread and a thin one look identical until you price the break risk.

Base rate 2.4% — 39 breaks in 1,606 settled outcomes. A floor, not a ceiling: deals that die before reaching a definitive proxy never enter the sample.

Annualising flatters everything. Here is the one I sell, done properly.

A 3% merger spread held to the median 49-day close annualises to 24.6% a year. That number is worthless, and it is the number this whole business would normally be sold on.

24.6%a year — what a 3% spread over 49 days looks like when you annualise it
+1.38%per deal — the same trade after a 2.4% break rate, a 27% median loss when one breaks, 0.40% round-trip cost and 0.49% of forgone T-bill carry

Price a 1% spread the same way — still a healthy-looking annualised number — and it is −0.57% per deal. Break-even sits at 1.59%.

That is the whole method in one line: the annualised number is marketing, the expected value is the trade.

The almach audit

$2,895 to $257,000, checked

A Polymarket trader turned $2,895 into $257,000 and an article explained how. I pulled 478,021 of their on-chain events and checked.

The profit was real: +$221,120 over 97 days, t = 7.52. The mechanism everyone assumed was wrong.

A third wallet in the same study looked profitable until the same filter took $176,266 out of it — 72% of its apparent profit, nearly all of it in one day that had presented as its biggest. Those were redemptions of positions opened before my measurement window. An artifact I had to catch before it fooled me too.

A paper clone now tests the real mechanism forward, evaluator sealed before starting. It will not report a number until it has 1,000 windows and 14 days. It is at 161.

None of that is impressive on its own. The way I killed them is.

How the rig works

Measure the instrument before you trust it

Before searching for an effect I measure the smallest effect the data could detect. If what I am hunting is below that floor, I do not run the search.

Thresholds frozen before the run. Evaluators hashed and sealed so they cannot be adjusted after seeing results. Every book with a coin-flip control running beside it. Sample size counted in independent events rather than trades.

And when the method itself fails, that gets logged too.

  • Filtering on cumulative volume leaked the outcome into the filter and flipped a result from −8¢ to +4¢
  • Reusing one holdout inflated measured gains 3.2× and suppressed the small negatives that would have closed a project months earlier
  • Normalisation layers over a time axis are silent lookahead

All of which is why none of it has money on it.
