Things I've made. Most of them work right here on this page.

Abyss Warden — tower defence game set on the ocean floor. ~5,600 lines of vanilla JS, no engine, no assets: sprites are drawn in code, sounds synthesised in the browser. Play it (phones work).

Chess Exchange — prediction market on live chess.com games. When titled players start a blitz game a market opens on the result, an LMSR market maker sets prices, six bots trade off material and clock, and the real result settles it. Play money. Runs as a server, so no web demo yet.

Doorly — flight search that prices the journey door to door, tube/train/taxi legs included, not just the fare. Demo (fake fares, labelled as such). iOS build exists.

Chelsea Bikes — rebuilt chelseabikes.co.uk to pitch to the shop. Their site has the phone number stuck inside an image; mine has their real prices and works offline. Before/after, full prototype. Not pitched yet.

PowerLeash — Mac menu bar app. I measured Low Power Mode and found it throttles the CPU ~40% without touching the display refresh rate, so I built this instead: background apps get pushed onto the efficiency cores, the app in front stays fast. Download (Apple Silicon, unsigned — right click → Open).

Trading research — a backtesting rig with pre-registration, a one-shot holdout and FDR correction. Ran 90 ideas through it and all 90 failed, which I wrote up: 90 Hypotheses, Zero Edges (pdf). Also five small-account strategies taken to written verdicts (three dead), and live tournament models — the DataCrunch one is around 14th of 13,534. Longer notes here.

Smaller stuff — Resale Radar (Depop/Vinted margin maths with real fees and return rates), tp-watch (pings my phone when club night tickets restock), and a batch of SEO/newsletter experiments that each had kill criteria written before launch.
