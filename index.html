Abyss Warden

A tower defence game set on the ocean floor. I wrote it in plain JavaScript on canvas, about 5,600 lines, with no assets at all: every sprite is drawn in code and every sound is synthesised in the browser while you play. Five bosses, around 20 levels, works with touch. It's finished, and builds are packaged for itch.io, Newgrounds and a few other portals.

→ Play it in your browser (phones work too)

Chess Exchange new this week

A play-money prediction market on real chess.com games. When titled players start a blitz game, a market opens on the result, priced off their Elo gap and draw rates. When the actual game ends, the market resolves and winning shares pay out. There's an automated market maker so you can always trade, plus six bots with different personalities that price replays off material and the clock, so a blunder makes the odds jump like it would on Polymarket. No frameworks, no real money.

It runs as a live server so I can't embed it in this static site. Hosted demo coming; ask me for a walkthrough in the meantime.

Doorly

Flight search that prices the whole journey door to door, because a cheap fare from an airport that costs £30 and two hours to reach isn't cheap. You type where you're starting and where you're staying, pick how you'd get to and from each airport (tube, train, coach, taxi, bike), and it re-ranks everything live. There's an iOS build too.

→ Try the demo (demo fares, clearly labelled; the real thing pulls live prices)

PowerLeash

A Mac menu bar app. I originally wanted to stop Low Power Mode dropping my screen to 60Hz. Then I measured it: Low Power Mode never touches the screen, it just throttles the CPU by roughly 40%, which is why everything feels worse while the panel happily runs at 120Hz. So I built what I actually wanted instead. It pushes background apps onto the efficiency cores (via the same public API as taskpolicy -b) and leaves whatever you're looking at running at full speed.

→ Download (Apple Silicon, unsigned for now: right-click → Open the first time)

Trading research

The long version has its own page. The short version: I built a backtesting rig designed to stop me fooling myself (every hypothesis pre-registered before touching data, a one-shot holdout, false-discovery correction across everything ever tested), then ran 90 ideas through it. All 90 failed. I wrote that up as an essay because a documented zero is worth more than an undocumented maybe.

Separately, I took five "works at small size" strategies to written verdicts (three dead, one honest upper bound, one small but real, now running), and I keep live models in a few ML tournaments. The DataCrunch one is currently ranked about 14th of 13,534.

Smaller things

  • Resale Radar. Tracks Depop/Vinted/eBay listings over time and models the actual fees and return rates, so "is this item worth stocking" gets a number instead of a guess. Saves get weighted well above likes; a save is someone planning to buy, a like is a bookmark.
  • tp-watch. Watches club night ticket pages in Exeter and pings my phone the moment something changes. Two halves: my laptop polls every 20 seconds when awake, a cloud copy covers 3am drops, and a deadman switch tells me if the watcher itself dies.
  • Trying new things. A batch of small internet experiments (programmatic SEO sites, an audit service, a newsletter). The rule I set myself: the conditions for killing each one were written down before it launched.
