The research log: one long argument with myself about what's real.

Everything on this page is connected. A trading bot taught me how easy it is to fool yourself; I built infrastructure that makes that impossible by construction; the infrastructure correctly found that 90 of my ideas were worthless; a separate validation effort killed three more strategies with arithmetic; the one survivor got execution tooling; and the same discipline, pointed at data-science tournaments, produced a top-0.2% model. Failures are documented with the same care as wins — that's the point.

02 — The instrument

Hypothesis Factory

Infrastructure for testing hundreds of trading hypotheses without fooling yourself. The core problem: at a 5% significance level, 100 uncorrected backtests hand you ~5 false discoveries by pure chance — and they look exactly like real edges: clean equity curves, good p-values, plausible stories. The factory's job is not running backtests; it is making self-deception structurally impossible. Four non-negotiables, each enforced by code rather than good intentions:

  • Pre-registration before any data is touched. Claim, direction, universe, holding period and success criterion are recorded to an append-only, hash-chained log. Editing, deleting or reordering any row breaks the chain, which every tool verifies before trusting anything. A failed hypothesis counts against the correction forever.
  • FDR correction, automatic and unavoidable. Benjamini–Hochberg is recomputed across every hypothesis ever tested, on every read. The report never shows a raw p-value without its adjusted counterpart on the same line — enforced by test.
  • Sealed holdout, one shot per hypothesis. History splits at a fixed calendar date. A hypothesis touches the holdout exactly once, and the attempt is burned to a ledger before the test runs — so a crash still consumes it. "It crashed, run it again" is a re-test loophole, and it is closed.
  • No parameter sweeping — enforced, not documented. A list or range as a parameter is refused at registration as the signature of a sweep. Three variants of an idea are three registrations counting three times against FDR. That's the correct accounting, not an inconvenience.

The factory also refuses to pretend about data quality: universes must declare whether they are survivorship-free (with free data, most can't be — so results are stamped SURVIVORSHIP-BIASED: UPPER BOUND rather than presented as estimates), and every field carries a known-as-of date so restated fundamentals can't leak the future into the past.

What it found: nothing — and that's the result

90 pre-registered hypotheses went through the machine. Zero survived. The system worked exactly as designed: it correctly refused to hand me a false edge to lose money on. The full audit trail became a 6,600-word essay.

04 — The survivor, taken live

uw-capture Live

The odd-lot tender strategy — the one verdict worth acting on — turned into execution tooling. Because the edge is small and per-deal, the engineering is mostly about not losing it to operational error:

  • Broker preflight checks and API probes for both Alpaca and Trading 212 (fractional-share behaviour, FX handling, auth) before any order path is trusted.
  • Guard tests on both broker integrations — the test suite exists to stop the code placing an order the strategy didn't ask for.
  • Paper-forward tracking first: the pipeline runs and records as if live, building a verifiable record before capital is committed.
  • Per-deal cost modelling and basket verification, plus scheduled monitoring via launchd.
05 — Where the discipline came from

Trading Bot Retired

The earliest project here, and the most instructive. It started as a market bot and became an exercise in finding out why its backtests were too good. The repo's table of contents tells the story better than a summary could: HONEST_FILL_BASELINE, FILL_REALITY_RESULT, CROSS_ARB_STOPLOSS_FINDING, BACKTEST_CALIBRATION_REPORT, EDGE_REMEDIATION_REPORT, and finally AUTOPSY_MAIN_ENGINE.

Each audit round found real problems — optimistic fill assumptions, costs that existed in the market but not in the simulator. Rather than patch around them, the project was retired and its lessons were rebuilt as constraints in Hypothesis Factory: everything the bot let me get away with, the factory now refuses.

06 — Blocked, honestly

ETF Selector Blocked

A total-cost-of-ownership optimiser for ETFs — not a return ranker. Three modules: can each required field be sourced free, what is the expected annual cost drag decomposed per residency, and what is the realised risk and correlation (measured, never forecast).

It currently ranks nothing, deliberately. The free-data availability audit found the cost stack can't be completed: spreads are sourceable for most funds, but TER needs per-issuer documents, tracking difference needs licensed index data, and point-in-time fund universes don't exist free. Ranking on a partial stack would put the fund with the least data on top rather than the least cost — so instead of a confident wrong answer, the tool reports exactly what is missing per fund.
