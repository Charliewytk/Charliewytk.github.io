Research notes

Around 110 hypotheses tested. Nine produced a real, measurable anomaly. Zero produced one bigger than the cost of trading it.

updated 27 aug 2026 · every number below says what kind of number it is — live, holdout, or backtest

The finding

what ~110 tests actually established

The result everyone expects from this kind of work is "nothing worked". That is not what happened. Nine anomalies came back real and measurable — and each was approximately the size of the costs required to trade it. The edge exists; it is spent getting to it. Separately, four findings survived independent replication, and none of them is a tradeable edge — which is the point.

That distinction matters, because it changes what to do next. A null says stop looking. A friction-sized edge says the search space is fine and the problem is execution, capacity or the cost base — which are things you can attack.

The second reason for the rig below: test enough ideas and some look brilliant purely by luck. They are not fakes — that is just what randomness looks like when you go hunting for patterns.

The rig

backtesting infrastructure · python

Four rules, enforced by the code rather than my good intentions:

  • Every idea is written down before any data is touched, in a log where editing an old entry breaks the chain.
  • The multiple-testing correction is applied automatically, every time results are read — so a failure from months ago still counts against a claim I make today.
  • The final test period is sealed. One attempt per idea, and the attempt is recorded before the test runs — so a crash still uses it up.
  • Parameter fishing is refused. Three versions of an idea count as three tests.

Ninety ideas went through it. None survived — written up as 90 Hypotheses, Zero Edges (pdf) — written at an earlier count.

That was the strategy-validation era. The ledger has since been restarted as a running record across the competition platforms I now enter — one row per hypothesis tested, failures especially, never reset, 32 rows so far and climbing. A post-mortem across the whole record found the deaths stack into three causes: most ideas die on economics, not statistics (the effect is real and roughly the size of its own trading costs); the next batch die of identification failure (the "signal" turns out to be market beta or cash yield wearing an alpha label); and event-driven ideas on free data hit an observability wall — free data deletes exactly the observations that would falsify the thesis.

Where it pays, and what it pays

merger monitor · the only thing with a price on it

Every US cash merger that has reached a definitive proxy, priced against the live market, carrying the sentence from the SEC filing the deal price came from. Repriced nightly. Break-even spread today is 1.59% and eleven of the sixteen clear it. The rest are published anyway, because a wide spread and a thin one look identical until you price the break risk.

Base rate: 2.4% of deals break after a definitive proxy — 39 of 1,606 settled outcomes. The page says out loud that this is a floor rather than a ceiling, because deals that collapse before that stage never enter the sample.

Why the annualised number is the wrong number

A 3% spread held to the median 49-day close annualises to 24.6% a year. That is how this kind of thing is normally sold, and it is worthless. Price the same trade properly — 2.4% break rate, 27% median loss when one does break, 0.40% round-trip cost, 0.49% of forgone Treasury-bill carry — and it is worth +1.38% per deal.

A 1% spread still annualises to something that looks healthy. Priced the same way it is −0.57% per deal. Break-even sits at 1.59%, which is why that number, and not the annualised one, is what the product publishes.

Structural break — 263rd of 1,332, and why that is the interesting part

57.43% live, against a leader at 65.24%. But the rank is not the finding. Two shipped improvements gained +0.0144 on holdout and delivered −0.0003 live. No holdout gain has ever transferred on this competition: zero for two.

The reasoning error is written down verbatim in my own submission note. Holdout levels had matched live levels twice before, and I used that to justify confidence in a holdout improvement. Those are different claims. The two prior live points were in fact evidence against delta transfer and I read them as evidence for it. The data to falsify that existed before I spent the quota. About 69% of the headline turned out to be selection inflation on a reused holdout.

WorldQuant BRAIN — seven alphas live

Three days and 308 single-term simulations produced a 0-for-5 rejection queue. Every candidate strong enough to clear the performance gates was a fast price/volume signal already in my book, so each one drew self-correlation around 0.79 against a 0.70 limit. Searching harder inside that class could not fix it, because the class was the problem.

Pairing each strong-but-crowded fast term with a weak, orthogonal slow one cleared 14 of 30 combinations, against 2 of 308 singles. Correlation fell 0.79 → 0.51 and fitness rose 1.04 → 1.68. The components that made it work had all failed on their own and would have been filed as dead ends.

The Almach study

$2,895 → +$221,120, audited from the chain

A Polymarket account was posting returns that looked impossible. Rather than guess, I pulled its complete on-chain ledger — 478,021 events over 97 days — and reconstructed every position.

The profit is real: +$221,120 from $2,895 of peak capital, t = 7.52. A second account independently returned +$291,481 at t = 10.15. Both win about half their bets at a median entry around 45–46¢, which is the tell: this is directional value betting on five-minute markets, not the market-making everyone assumed it was.

Knowing the mechanism is not the same as being able to run it, so there is now a paper clone with a sealed evaluator and a verdict due around 10 September. Nothing real is staked on it.

How the rig works

the part nobody else publishes

Every trading blog has results. Almost none can tell you what they could have detected. That is the difference, and it is the part I would defend hardest:

  • The detection floor is measured before the search runs. On Quantiacs the floor was 0.68 while the real effects were 0.1–0.2 — and taking the best of a hundred tries manufactured a +0.30 that was pure selection. Knowing the floor first is what stops that being written up as a finding.
  • Thresholds are pre-registered and the evaluator is sealed. One attempt per idea, recorded before the test runs, so a crash still spends it.
  • Null control books run alongside the real ones. If the null scores, the instrument is broken, not the market.
  • Effective sample size, not trade counts. Sixty games from thirty seeds are not sixty samples, and row counts flatter entities by three to eight times across the whole record.
  • Traps are logged when the method fails, not just when the idea does: volume filters that introduced lookahead, a reused holdout that inflated results 3.2×, normalisation leaking across time.

Three findings from that discipline outrank any single strategy. Edges are friction-sized. Reproducibility is not relevance — four platforms shipped locally reproducible gains that scored roughly zero live. And matching levels licenses nothing about improvements.
