Research notes

This is the trading and ML side of what I do, in one place. It's mostly a story about learning not to trust my own backtests, and what I built once I stopped trusting them. The failures are documented as carefully as the wins, because the failures are where the method came from.

ADIA structural break challenge

Detecting regime changes in time series. My entry is a GRU ensemble over 46 features (26 streaming, 20 changepoint statistics), trained across 5 seeds with a strict early-stop protocol where the holdout gets touched exactly once per artefact. The ensemble scores 0.5696 on holdout against a 0.5588 GBM baseline; the individual seeds range 0.5579 to 0.5664.

Numerai

A single-file pipeline: download, train, validate, predict, submit, targeting their v5.3 dataset, plus their model-upload path. One detail I'm fond of: their scoring container runs Python 3.12, and building the pickle on any other minor version fails as a segfault inside their container rather than an error message, so the pipeline pins the version and I confirmed the container image through their API. I deliberately don't stake money on it yet. Live scores build a public track record first; capital only goes in if the record earns it.

The hypothesis rig

The core problem with backtesting lots of ideas: at a 5% significance level, 100 uncorrected tests will hand you about 5 false edges by pure chance, and they look exactly like real ones. Clean equity curves, good p-values, plausible stories. So I built infrastructure where fooling myself is structurally hard, not just discouraged:

  • Every hypothesis is registered before any data gets touched: the claim, direction, universe, holding period and pass criterion, timestamped into an append-only log that's hash-chained, so editing or deleting any row breaks verification for everything after it. A failed idea counts against me forever.
  • Benjamini-Hochberg correction is recomputed across every hypothesis ever tested, every time results are read. No raw p-value is ever shown without its corrected value next to it. There's a test that enforces this.
  • The holdout is sealed behind a fixed calendar date and each hypothesis gets one shot at it. The attempt is written to a ledger before the test runs, so a crash still burns it. "It crashed, run it again" is a retesting loophole and it's closed.
  • Parameter sweeps are refused at registration. If you want three variants of an idea, that's three registrations, and all three count against the correction.

It also refuses to pretend about data quality. Free data can't make most stock universes survivorship-free (yfinance only knows tickers that exist today, so every delisting and bankruptcy is silently missing), so instead of pretending otherwise, results from biased universes get stamped as upper bounds. Every field carries a known-as-of date so restated fundamentals can't leak the future into the past.

I ran 90 registered hypotheses through it. Zero survived. That's the rig working, not failing: it refused to hand me a fake edge to lose money on. The full audit trail became a 6,600 word essay, 90 Hypotheses, Zero Edges (pdf).

uw-capture

The odd-lot tender idea survived, so it got execution tooling. The edge is small and per-deal, which means the engineering is mostly about not losing it to operational mistakes: preflight checks against both brokers (Alpaca and Trading 212) before any order path is trusted, guard tests whose whole job is stopping the code from placing an order the strategy didn't ask for, and a paper-forward phase that runs everything as if live to build a verifiable record before real money moves.

Where the discipline came from

The earliest project here was a trading bot, and it's the most instructive one because of how it ended. Its backtests kept looking too good, and each audit round found out why: optimistic fill assumptions, costs that existed in the market but not the simulator. The repo filenames tell the story on their own: HONEST_FILL_BASELINE, FILL_REALITY_RESULT, BACKTEST_CALIBRATION_REPORT, EDGE_REMEDIATION_REPORT, and eventually AUTOPSY_MAIN_ENGINE. I retired it and rebuilt the lessons as hard constraints in the hypothesis rig. Everything the bot let me get away with, the rig now refuses.

ETF selector, currently blocked

A total-cost-of-ownership tool for ETFs that deliberately ranks nothing right now. The free-data audit found the cost stack can't be completed (tracking difference needs licensed index data, TER needs per-issuer documents, and no free source lists funds that have closed), and ranking on a partial stack would put the fund with the least data on top rather than the least cost. So instead of a confident wrong answer it reports exactly what's missing for each fund. Blocked is the honest status.
